Calorie & Nutrition Resources

Calculators, databases, and official guidance worth your time. Everything here comes from a US government health or agriculture agency.

⚠️ When calorie tracking needs clinical help

Talk to a clinician rather than adjusting your intake further if you notice:
• Restricting, bingeing, or purging, or distress that dominates your day
• Rapid unintended weight loss you did not set out to cause
• Fainting, dizziness, or missed periods while dieting
• Tracking that has become compulsive and hard to stop

Eating disorder support: call or text the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, or speak with your primary care clinician.

Guidelines & Official Guidance

Dietary Guidelines for Americans, 2025-2030

The current edition, released January 2026 by USDA and HHS. Short enough to read in one sitting, and the basis for federal nutrition programs.

Free • Government source • PDF

Steps for Losing Weight (CDC)

The CDC's guidance on gradual weight loss, including why 1 to 2 pounds a week is the target and how activity and intake work together.

Free • Government source

Calculators & Databases

NIH Body Weight Planner

A validated model from NIDDK that estimates the intake and activity needed to reach a goal weight and then maintain it. More realistic than fixed-formula calculators because it accounts for metabolic adaptation.

Free • Government source

USDA FoodData Central

The USDA's reference database for the nutrient content of foods. The source most other calorie databases are ultimately derived from.

Free • Government source • Searchable
